# Contents

The USB drive contains a mixture of personal and work-related information, It includes family and pet photos, travel plans, wedding documents, employee schedules, budgeting information, and hiring documents. Some of these files may contain personally identifiable information PII and sensitive organizational data that  could be useful to an attacker


# Attacker Mindset

An attacker could use the information stored on the USB drive to perform social engineering attacks against Jorge or the hospital.Personal details, travel plans, employee schedules, and hiring information could be used to craft convincing phishing messages or impersonate trusted individuals. The USB drive itself could also be used as bait to introduce malicious software into the hospital network.


# Risk analysis

Organization can reduce the risk of USB baiting attacks through technical, operational, and managerial controls. 
Employees should receive security awareness training that teaches them not to connect unknown USB devices to organization systems. Endpoint protection tools, device control policies, and antivirus software can help block malicious USB devices.
Additionally, organizations can restrict USB usage, implement least privilege principles, and require suspicious devices to be analyzed in an isolated virtual environment before being accessed.
