# Definition

Concept| Definition|
-------|-----------|
Social Engineering | Manipulating people into revealing information or performing actions that compromise security
Human factor | The tendency for people to trust, help, or react emotionally, which attackers exploit
Pretext | A fabricated scenario used to gain trust

# Four Stages of Social Engineering

Stage | Description|
------|------------|
1. Prepare | Gather information about the target
2. Establish Trust | Build credibility and rapport
3. Use Persuasion Tactics | Manipulate the target into taking action
4. Disconnect | End communication and avoid detection

# Common Social Engineering Attacks

Attack Type | Description | Delivery Method
------------|-------------|----------------|
Phishing | Fraudulent messages designed to steal information| Email
Smishing | Phishing using text message (SMS)| SMS
Vishing | Phishing using voice calls | Telephone
Whaling | Phishing targeting executives or high-valur individuals| Email
Spear Phishing| Highly targeted phishing attack |Email
Pretexting |Creating a fake scenario to obtain information | Email, Phone, In-Person
Baiting | Luring victims with something attractive | USB, Download
Quid Pro Quo | Offering a service or benefit in exchange for information | Phone, Email
Tailgating | Following an authorized person into a restricted area | Physical Access

# Phishing Family

Attack | Example|
-------|--------|
Phishing | Fake bank email requesting account verificaiton
Smishing | Text message claiming a package delivery problem
Vishing | Call pretending to be technical support
Spear Phishing | Email targeting a specific employee
Whaling | Email targeting executives or senior management


# Social Engineering Tactics

Tactic | Purpose|
-------|--------|
Urgency | force quick decisions
Fear | Create anxiety or panic
Authority | Impersonate trusted individuals
Trust | Build credibility
Curiosity | Encourage interaction
Helpfulness | Exploit willingness to assist

# Social Engineering and Threat Modeling

Question | Example|
---------|--------|
Who is the target? | Employee, Customer, Executive
What information is valuable? | Credentials, Financial Data, PII
How could the attacker reach the target? | Email, SMS, Phone, Social Media
What defenses should exist? | Training, MFA, Policies

Suspicious

Indicator
Unsolicited text message
Urgent action requested
Shortened URLs
Requests for personal information

## Vishing
Indicator
Caller pressures immediate action
Requests credentials or payments
Claims to represent a trusted organization

# Security Controls Against Social Engineering

Control| Purpose|
-------|--------|
Security Awareness Training| Teach users how attacks work
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) | Reduce impact of credential theft
Email Security Filters | Detect phishing attempts
Verification Procedures | Confirm identity before sharing information
Least Privilege |Limit damage from compromised accounts
Reporting Mechanisms | Allow users to respect suspicious messages
